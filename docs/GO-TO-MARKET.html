The cause of zero, found and fixed

Registration worked perfectly and returned 201 every time it was tested. It was simply unreachable: eleven marketing pages, and not one had a primary call to action that arrived at an account. The nav pointed at an onboarding explainer that ended in “Request access” pointing at a mailto: form.

This matters more than any channel choice. It means the absence of customers has never been evidence about demand — the experiment was never run. Every number in this document is a first measurement, not a recovery.

02

Positioning, and the one sentence

What you are, to whom, against what alternative.

Every fitness app sells transformation. That is a crowded, expensive, low-trust category where the incumbent is a competitor’s brand budget and the customer’s own failed January. Jess Move should not enter it.

The defensible position is the opposite claim: this is not a fitness product, it is a product about the day you already have. Two minutes. Between things. Ages ten to a hundred. That framing wins in exactly the places a fitness app is refused — a falls group, a care home, a school, a link worker’s caseload.

The sentence Jess Move finds the movement your day already has room for — two minutes at a time, for anyone from ten to a hundred — and it never tells you you’re fine when you’re not.
To a care professional It gives someone a starting level, not a risk score. It says out loud that a good result isn’t evidence of low risk. And nobody is paid to recommend it.
To an employer Group reporting with a floor of eight people. You see whether a programme is working. You never see a person.

The three claims that do the work

Claim 01 — refusals

It says what it will not do

A published hazard log, enforced refusals, and a strength module that states a good score is not evidence of low risk. In health, the product that under-claims is the one a professional can pass on.

Claim 02 — no conflict

Nobody is paid to refer

No commission, no per-signup fee, no revenue share. This is the single most valuable asset you own for clinical and public-sector channels, where a fee is a disqualifying conflict of interest.

Claim 03 — the household

Ten to a hundred, one account

Family at £12.99 covers four people across child and later-life modes with guardian controls. No competitor in falls prevention also serves a ten-year-old.

What not to say

Never lead with the AI

No vendor or model names on public pages — a standing rule, and correct. Buyers in this market hear “AI” as risk. Lead with the refusals; the intelligence is how, not what.

04

The launch city, locked

One city. Everything else is a distraction until it repeats.

A referral motion is a travel motion. Every room in this plan is a room you walk into, which means the launch city is not a market-sizing decision — it is a decision about how many rooms you can physically reach in a week.

Locked: Greater Manchester

Manchester is the UK’s first age-friendly city, admitted to the WHO Global Network for Age-Friendly Cities in June 2010, with a dedicated older people’s programme running since 2003 and a current strategy, Manchester: A city for life 2023–28. Greater Manchester became the first age-friendly city region in the UK, and in February 2025 launched a case-finding falls-prevention pilot supported by the Centre for Ageing Better.

That matters for one specific reason: the rooms already exist and are already funded. You are not creating demand for organised movement among older people in Manchester — a public programme has spent two decades creating it. You are arriving at the moment those programmes end, which is the gap nobody funds.

The one assumption behind this lock

That you can be in Greater Manchester most weeks. This is the only input to the decision I do not have. If you are based elsewhere, the answer changes to your own city immediately — a founder two hours from the rooms will book three a month instead of three a week, and the plan fails on travel rather than on product.

The selection criteria are what matter, and they are portable: a funded ageing or falls programme, a single integrated care system, a public transport network that puts three rooms within a day, and housing providers with local stock. Apply them to where you live and relock.

The first 100 customers

Counted properly — a registration and a payment are not the same event.

A correction to the earlier version of this plan

The first draft counted “accounts” as though a person leaving a room became a paying customer. They do not. Free has no card and lasts forever; the AI allowance is 50 ACU a month for two months. So a room produces registrations, and a separate conversion produces payments — and 100 paying accounts needs roughly 45 rooms, not 17.

Read the last column before you celebrate the fourth

A hundred paying customers is £785 a month. That is not a business, and pretending otherwise is how founders raise on the wrong story. What ninety days buys is not revenue — it is a proven, repeatable room: a named contact, a known take-up rate, and a conversion number you measured rather than modelled.

That asset is what makes month four to twelve possible, and what makes an organisation contract or an investor conversation defensible. Sell the mechanism, not the MRR.

If the first three rooms register below 15% of the people in them, the problem is the handover moment or the offer — and no number of extra rooms fixes it. Stop and change the offer before booking room four. This costs three weeks and about £60 of travel to learn.

The 30 / 60 / 90

Each phase has one job and one gate. Do not start the next until the gate passes.

DAY 1 – 30

Make it possible to pay you

  • Days 1–7 · Unblock. SMTP credentials in Vercel. Stripe live keys and price IDs. Run the AI provider probe. Confirm the deploy is past the branch head.
  • Days 3–10 · Buy your own product. From a phone, as a stranger, on mobile data. Pay real money. Cancel. Refund yourself.
  • Days 5–14 · Appoint a Clinical Safety Officer. Longest lead time of anything here.
  • Days 7–21 · Book eight rooms across Greater Manchester. Falls groups and link workers only.
  • Days 10–30 · Run six. Observe, do not pitch. Record take-up per room.
  • Ongoing · Approve one newsletter and two articles weekly. Ninety minutes.
Gate to phase 2 Money settled in the bank from a stranger. Six rooms run. Registration take-up above 15% and a funnel that shows where the rest stopped.
DAY 31 – 60

Prove the room repeats

  • Repeat the winning room type. Whichever of the six converted best, book twelve more of exactly that.
  • Fix the worst funnel step. You will have real drop-off data; one screen will own most of the loss.
  • Open two organisation conversations. Sheltered housing and one care provider. Expect nothing before day 90.
  • Publish the falls content cluster. Twelve articles aimed at what an adult child types at 11pm.
  • Ask your first twenty registrants: “What nearly stopped you?”
  • Watch the two-month ACU cliff hit your earliest cohort. This is your first real conversion signal.
Gate to phase 3 Twenty rooms run. Free-to-paid conversion measured, not modelled. Month-one retention above 60%. One organisation proposal on the table.
DAY 61 – 90

Turn one room into a channel

  • Recruit five standing referrers — people who hand it on every cohort, not once.
  • Close one organisation contract. Ten seats minimum, annual. Any logo beats a bigger pipeline.
  • Build the referrer pack. One page a professional can hand over without explaining anything.
  • Instrument every code. Nine referrer kinds already exist — give each room type its own.
  • Decide on app stores. £8.99 versus £5.99 is a 33% margin question, not a distribution one.
  • Write the case study. One named Manchester group, real numbers, reviewed by them.
Gate to scaling spend 45 rooms run. Payback under 60 days, measured. A repeatable room type with a named contact who will do it again unprompted.

What is deliberately absent from all ninety days

No paid advertising. No app-store launch. No press. No conference stand. No influencer. Each spends money to find people you cannot yet keep, before you know which screen loses them. Paid acquisition begins on day 91 at the earliest, and only if payback under 60 days has been measured rather than assumed.

Sourcing principle

Before signing any supplier, ask: “Does this become a question I have to answer in every future sale?” A processor holding health data does. A pen-test report does not — it answers questions. Buy the second kind freely and the first kind almost never.

What the budget will not return inside 90 days

At the committed target of 97 paying customers you finish the quarter at roughly £785 MRR against £16,940 spent. On a pure cash view that is twenty-two months to recover, and if you judge the quarter that way you will conclude wrongly that it failed.

Most of the £16,940 is not acquisition spend — it is one-off market access. The CSO, the DPIA, Cyber Essentials and the accessibility audit do not repeat, and they unlock a channel where a single 100-seat contract at £4 is £4,800 a year from one signature. The recurring cost of this plan is £373 a month, and the acquisition cost inside it is £1,620 for 45 rooms — £36 a room, or roughly £17 per paying customer. That number is the one to judge.

If you can only spend £2,760

Take the lean tier and do it properly. Twenty rooms, consumer only, no organisation conversations at all. You will finish the quarter with about 36–45 paying customers, a measured conversion rate and a repeatable room — which is exactly the evidence needed to justify the committed spend in quarter two, out of revenue or out of a raise. The one thing not to do is spend half of tier D: a DPIA with no CSO opens nothing.

The organisation-pricing trap

Ten seats at the £2 floor is £20 a month — less than three consumer subscribers, for a sale that takes three months and a DPIA. The minimum viable organisation deal is closer to fifty seats, or £5 per seat. Price the floor for schools and charities where the logo is worth more than the revenue; quote everyone else at £4–5 and let the contract close the gap.

The free tier, read as an acquisition instrument

Free forever with no card, and 50 ACU a month of AI for two months. This is unusually well judged for this market: the people you are selling to are frequently burned by subscriptions they could not cancel, and “no card, nothing charged” removes the single biggest objection a link worker raises on a customer’s behalf.

The risk is the cliff. In month three the AI allowance stops. Instrument that moment specifically — it is your only real conversion event. If conversion at the cliff is under 20%, the fix is a smaller permanently-free AI allowance, not a longer trial.

10

Marketing engine

Four surfaces, already built. None of them is advertising.

Surface 01 — owned

The falls content cluster

Write for the adult child at 11pm, not the clinician. “Exercises to stop falling”, “my mum is scared of falling”, “what to eat on appetite-suppressing medication”. Twelve articles beats a hundred thin ones. Every article must reach registration in one click.

Surface 02 — earned

The assurance page

A published hazard log and a list of enforced refusals lets a buyer diligence you at midnight without booking a meeting. This shortens institutional sales more than any brochure. Link it in the first email of every organisation conversation.

Surface 03 — retained

The weekly newsletter

Composed from your own feature pages, so it cannot promise something that does not exist. Its job is not acquisition — it is stopping the month-two drop-off by showing people the product they already have.

Surface 04 — the multiplier

The referrer pack

One page. What it does, what it refuses to do, the privacy answer, and a code. A professional will not deliver your pitch — they will hand over a piece of paper. Make that paper excellent and the channel scales without you in the room.

On the agency recommendation — marketwaros.com

I could not evaluate this. The site is unreachable from this environment: the network policy refused the request outright, and a web search returns no such domain (the near matches are MarketWatch, marketwars.com and MarketWurks, none of which appear to be what you mean).

Rather than invent a recommendation: at this stage you should probably not hire an agency at all. You have no conversion data, so an agency would be optimising a funnel nobody has measured, and you would pay several thousand pounds to learn what your first three rooms tell you for free. Nothing in the budget above is allocated to one. If you engage one anyway, require named UK health or public-sector clients you can phone, a refusal to promise leads, payment tied to a defined deliverable rather than a monthly retainer, and no access to member data of any kind.
