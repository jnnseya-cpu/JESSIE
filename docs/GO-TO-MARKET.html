Registration worked perfectly and returned 201 every time it was tested. It was simply unreachable: eleven marketing pages, and not one had a primary call to action that arrived at an account. The nav pointed at an onboarding explainer that ended in “Request access” pointing at a mailto: form.

Four things gate revenue, and none is a marketing problem

SMTP credentials, a live Stripe path, working AI keys, and a Clinical Safety Officer. Until those four are closed, spending on acquisition converts interest into refunds and apologies. Days 1–14 exist to close them.

02

Positioning, and the one sentence

What you are, to whom, against what alternative.

Every fitness app sells transformation. That is a crowded, expensive, low-trust category where the incumbent is Strava’s brand budget and the customer’s own failed January. Jess Move should not enter it.

The defensible position is the opposite claim: this is not a fitness product, it is a product about the day you already have. Two minutes. Between things. Ages ten to a hundred. That framing wins in exactly the places a fitness app is refused — a falls group, a care home, a school, a link worker’s caseload.

The sentence Jess Move finds the movement your day already has room for — two minutes at a time, for anyone from ten to a hundred — and it never tells you you’re fine when you’re not.
To a care professional It gives someone a starting level, not a risk score. It says out loud that a good result isn’t evidence of low risk. And nobody is paid to recommend it.
To an employer Group reporting with a floor of eight people. You see whether a programme is working. You never see a person.

The three claims that do the work

Claim 01 — refusals

It says what it will not do

A published hazard log, enforced refusals, and a strength module that states a good score is not evidence of low risk. In health, the product that under-claims is the one a professional can pass on.

Claim 02 — no conflict

Nobody is paid to refer

No commission, no per-signup fee, no revenue share. This is the single most valuable asset you own for clinical and public-sector channels, where a fee would be a disqualifying conflict of interest.

Claim 03 — the whole household

Ten to a hundred, one account

Family at £12.99 covers four people across child and later-life modes with guardian controls. No competitor in falls prevention also serves a ten-year-old.

What not to say

Never the AI

No vendor or model names on public pages — a standing rule, and correct. Buyers in this market hear “AI” as risk. Lead with the refusals; the intelligence is how, not what.

The strategic read

Ranks 1, 2 and 4 are consumer revenue reached through an institutional door. That is the whole trick: you get the trust and the concentration of a B2B channel while selling a £5.99 consumer subscription that needs no procurement, no contract, no information-governance review, and no Clinical Safety Officer.

Ranks 3, 6, 7 and 8 are real contracts worth ten to a hundred times more each — and they take a quarter to close. Start them in month one, expect them in month four. Do not let their slowness stop you counting the fast ones.

Why the take-up rates are plausible, and how to falsify them fast

These are modelled, not measured — you have no conversion history. They assume a warm, in-person handover from a trusted professional at a moment of live need, which is the highest-converting acquisition context that exists outside of search intent.

Falsify by room three. If the first three rooms convert below 15%, the problem is the handover moment or the price, not the volume — and no amount of extra rooms fixes it. Stop and change the offer before booking room four.

05

The 30 / 60 / 90

Each phase has one job and one gate. Do not start the next until the gate passes.

DAY 1 – 30

Make it possible to pay you

  • Days 1–7 · Unblock. SMTP credentials in Vercel. Stripe live keys and price IDs. Run the AI provider probe. Confirm the deploy is past the branch head.
  • Days 3–10 · Buy your own product. From a phone, as a stranger, on mobile data. Pay real money. Cancel. Refund yourself.
  • Days 5–14 · Appoint a Clinical Safety Officer. Longest lead time of anything here.
  • Days 7–21 · Book six rooms. Falls groups and link workers only.
  • Days 10–30 · Run three. Observe, do not pitch. Record the take-up.
  • Ongoing · Approve one newsletter and two articles weekly. Ninety minutes total.
Gate to phase 2 Ten paying accounts, acquired from at most three rooms, with money actually settled in the bank — and the funnel showing where the other people stopped.
DAY 31 – 60

Prove the room repeats

  • Repeat the winning room type. Whichever of the three converted best, book six more of exactly that.
  • Fix the worst funnel step. You will have real drop-off data. One screen will be responsible for most of the loss.
  • Open two organisation conversations. Sheltered housing and one care provider. Expect nothing before day 90.
  • Publish the falls content cluster. Twelve articles targeting what an adult child types at 11pm.
  • Ask your first twenty customers one question: “What nearly stopped you?”
  • Turn on the weekly newsletter properly. Consent grows from the account page.
Gate to phase 3 Forty paying accounts. Month-one retention above 60%. At least one organisation with a written proposal on the table.
DAY 61 – 90

Turn one room into a channel

  • Recruit five standing referrers. People who hand it on every cohort, not once.
  • Close one organisation contract. Ten seats minimum, annual. Any logo beats a bigger pipeline.
  • Build the referrer pack. One page a professional can hand over without explaining anything.
  • Instrument every code. Nine referrer kinds already exist — make each room use its own.
  • Decide on app stores. £8.99 versus £5.99 is a 33% margin question, not a distribution one.
  • Write the case study. One named group, real numbers, reviewed by them.
Gate to scaling spend One hundred accounts. Payback under 60 days. A repeatable room type with a named contact who will do it again unprompted.

What is deliberately absent from all ninety days

No paid advertising. No app-store launch. No press. No conference stand. No influencer. Every one of those spends money to find people you cannot yet keep, before you know which screen loses them. Paid acquisition begins on day 91 at the earliest, and only if payback under 60 days has been measured rather than assumed.

Sourcing principle

For every supplier above, ask one question before signing: “Does this become a question I have to answer in every future sale?” A processor holding health data does. A pen-test report does not — it answers questions. Buy the second kind freely and the first kind almost never.

Acquisition economics

The organisation-pricing trap

Ten seats at the £2 floor is £20 a month — less than four consumer subscribers, for a sale that takes three months and a DPIA. The minimum viable organisation deal is closer to fifty seats, or £5 per seat. Price the floor for schools and charities where the logo is worth more than the revenue; quote everyone else at £4–5 and let the contract close the gap.

The free tier, read as an acquisition instrument

Fifty ACU a month for two months, then never again — and no card. This is unusually well judged for this market. The people you are selling to are frequently burned by subscriptions they could not cancel, and “no card, nothing charged” removes the single biggest objection a link worker will raise on a customer’s behalf.

The risk is the cliff. Month three arrives and the allowance stops. Instrument that moment specifically: if conversion at the cliff is under 20%, the fix is a smaller permanently-free tier, not a longer trial.

08

Marketing engine

Three surfaces, already built. None of them is advertising.

Surface 01 — owned

The falls content cluster

Write for the adult child at 11pm, not the clinician. “Exercises to stop falling”, “my mum is scared of falling”, “what to eat on appetite-suppressing medication”. Twelve articles beats a hundred thin ones. Every article must reach registration in one click.

Surface 02 — earned

The assurance page

A published hazard log and a list of enforced refusals lets a buyer diligence you at midnight without booking a meeting. This shortens institutional sales more than any brochure. Link it from every organisation conversation in the first email.

Surface 03 — retained

The weekly newsletter

Composed from your own feature pages, so it cannot promise something that does not exist. Consent-gated and currently reaching five people. Its job is not acquisition — it is stopping the month-two drop-off by showing people the product they already paid for.

Surface 04 — the multiplier

The referrer pack

One page. What it does, what it refuses to do, the privacy answer, and a code. A professional will not deliver your pitch — they will hand over a piece of paper. Make that paper excellent and the channel scales without you in the room.

On the agency recommendation — marketwaros.com

I could not evaluate this. The site is unreachable from this environment: the network policy refused the request outright, and a web search returns no such domain (the near matches are MarketWatch, marketwars.com and MarketWurks, none of which appear to be what you mean).

The one number that matters most

Room take-up. Everything else is downstream. If a trusted professional hands your product to twelve people at the exact moment they need it and fewer than two sign up, no channel, no article and no advertisement will save the plan — and you will have learned it in three weeks for the cost of travel.
